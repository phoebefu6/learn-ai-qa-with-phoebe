Learn AI QA with Phoebe

Six sessions, 45 minutes each, and one test suite you can genuinely defend. Two suites, the same code under test, both green, both at one hundred percent line coverage. One catches three of the twelve ways that code can break and the other catches eleven. Coverage cannot tell them apart, and coverage is what your dashboard shows you. This course is about the metric that can.

The six sessions 🧪 · one real test suite, built across all of them

Bring a module you own. Small enough to read in a sitting, important enough that a silent bug in it would matter. By session 6 you have a suite you can defend and a gate your team can actually run.

🟢 Session 1 · easy

The green suite lie

Two suites, identical 100 percent coverage, mutation scores of 25 and 91.7. Run both yourself in the browser, then kill the last surviving mutant by hand. Real execution, nothing scripted.

▶ Start here ✔️ Session 2 · easy

What a test is for

Assertions are the thing that correlates with catching bugs, and there is a paper on it. Audit your own suite for tests that execute code and check nothing, plus the change-detector tests that break on every refactor.

assertions 🤖 Session 3 · medium

Generating tests

Copilot, Playwright's test agents, Vitest's own guidance, and the ordering problem: tests written after faulty code detect 14 percent of faults, tests written independently detect 25.

order matters 🧬 Session 4 · medium

Mutation testing

Properly this time: the operators, the tools, and the validation across 357 real faults. Plus the honest limit, because for 27 percent of those faults no mutant was coupled at all.

the real metric 🎲 Session 5 · hands-on

Flakes and speed

84 percent of pass-to-fail transitions at Google involve a flaky test. Flakiness scales with test size: small 0.5 percent, large 14. A suite nobody trusts is a suite nobody reads.

trust 🚦 Session 6 · hands-on

The quality gate

Write the gate your team runs on Monday. Mutation score on the diff, not the codebase. Coverage demoted to a floor detector. And a rule for generated tests that arrive by the hundred.

What is real here 🧾

Unusually for a course with a simulator, this one has no disclaimer to make.

ClaimStatus
Coverage is not strongly correlated with test suite effectivenessPeer-reviewed. Inozemtseva and Holmes, ICSE 2014, ACM Distinguished Paper, 31,000 suites
Assertions are strongly correlated with effectivenessPeer-reviewed. Zhang and Mesbah, ESEC/FSE 2015, 6,700 suites, 24,000 assertions
Mutants stand in for real faults, independently of coveragePeer-reviewed. Just et al., FSE 2014, 357 real faults. Its own limit is taught alongside it
The playground's coverage and mutation numbersReal execution of your own tests in your own browser. Not a model of anything
